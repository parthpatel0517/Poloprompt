Why Your Amazon Listing Won't Work on Etsy (And Vice Versa)

Sellers who list the same product on multiple marketplaces often copy and paste one listing into the next platform's editor, change a few words, and move on. Then they wonder why conversion rates differ wildly between platforms selling to what should be a similar audience. The reason is that each marketplace's shoppers arrive with different expectations, and the listing conventions on each platform have evolved specifically to match them. Copy-pasting ignores all of that.

Amazon: keyword-dense title, five-bullet structure

Amazon shoppers are typically in transactional mode — they searched for something specific and are comparing a page of similar results. Amazon's listing format reflects that: a long, keyword-front-loaded title that packs in the brand, product type, key specs, and size or quantity, followed by five scannable bullet points that each lead with a benefit in caps or bold-style phrasing before the supporting detail. There's little room for storytelling because Amazon shoppers are scanning fast, comparing several tabs at once, and deciding largely on price, star rating, and whether the bullets answer their specific question (does it fit, is it compatible, what's included).

Shopify: brand voice and on-site SEO

A Shopify store is the seller's own site, so shoppers arriving there have usually already been influenced by an ad, an email, or a search result that led with the brand rather than a generic product category. Listings on Shopify can and should carry more brand personality — consistent tone, a point of view, maybe a bit of story about why the product exists — while still being written with on-site SEO in mind, since organic search is often a bigger traffic source for independent stores than marketplace search is. A Shopify description that reads like an Amazon bullet list wastes the brand-building opportunity that owning the storefront actually gives you.

Etsy: story-driven, handmade tone

Etsy shoppers are shopping for something with a story attached — handmade, vintage, personalized, or small-batch — and the platform's top-performing listings lean into that instead of away from it. Etsy descriptions typically open with the material, the making process, or the inspiration behind the piece, use warmer and more personal language, and often address the buyer directly about how the item might be used or gifted. A keyword-stuffed, bullet-heavy Amazon-style listing feels cold and out of place here, and tends to underperform against competitors leaning into craft and personality.

Meesho: plain-language, price-sensitive, mobile-first

Meesho's buyer base skews toward highly price-conscious, mobile-first shoppers, many of whom are comparing near-identical products largely on price and delivery speed. Listings that perform well tend to use short, plain, easily scannable language (often bilingual or simple English), lead with price and value framing, and avoid dense paragraphs or the kind of brand-story language that works on Etsy or Shopify. A beautifully written, story-driven Etsy-style description would likely feel slow and irrelevant to a Meesho shopper trying to compare five near-identical listings on a small phone screen in under a minute.

Why copy-pasting underperforms

Each of these conventions developed because they match the mindset of the shopper on that specific platform — transactional comparison shopping on Amazon, brand affinity on Shopify, story and craft appreciation on Etsy, price-first scanning on Meesho. A listing tuned for one of these mindsets doesn't just look slightly off on another platform, it actively works against the way that platform's shoppers make decisions. The fix isn't to write four completely different products — it's to keep the same core facts (materials, dimensions, use case) and re-frame the presentation, tone, and structure for whichever shopper is actually reading it.
