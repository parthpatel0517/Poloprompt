How to Write Content That AI Search Tools Actually Cite

A growing share of questions never reach a traditional list of ten blue links. People ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity directly, or read an AI-generated summary at the top of the search results. Those answers usually cite a handful of sources, and being one of them is quickly becoming as valuable as ranking first.

There's no secret technique for getting cited. The pages that AI answers quote tend to have the same qualities as genuinely useful content, just applied more strictly. Here's what that means in practice.

How AI answers pick what to quote

Each tool works differently and none of them publish their full methods, but the general pattern is similar: find pages relevant to the question, then pull out the passages that answer it most directly. That second step is where most content loses. A clear, self-contained, specific paragraph is easy to lift and attribute. The same information spread across a rambling introduction, three anecdotes and a pop-up is not.

So the goal is simple to state: make the best answer on your page easy to find, easy to understand out of context, and obviously trustworthy.

1. Answer the question in the first two sentences

If your article targets "how long does sourdough starter take", the answer belongs at the top: "Most sourdough starters are ready to bake with in 7 to 14 days. You'll know yours is ready when it reliably doubles within 4 to 8 hours of feeding." Then explain the nuance. Long scene-setting introductions push the answer down, where it is less likely to be picked up.

2. Write headings the way people ask questions

"Troubleshooting" tells a reader very little. "Why is my starter not rising?" matches how the question is actually asked, both in a search box and in a chat. Question-shaped headings also make it obvious which paragraph answers which question.

3. Make every paragraph stand on its own

Quoted passages are read out of context, so phrases like "as mentioned above", "this method" or "the second option" break them. Repeat the noun instead: "The overnight method works best when...". It feels slightly redundant when you write it, but every paragraph becomes quotable.

4. Be specific: numbers, names and dates

"Prices vary a lot" can't be cited. "Most local bakeries charge between $8 and $12 for a sourdough loaf", with a source or your own survey behind it, can. Specific claims give an AI answer something concrete to quote and give readers a reason to trust you. Your own first-hand data, such as test results, before-and-after numbers or photos of your process, is especially valuable because nobody else has it.

5. Use lists and tables for steps and comparisons

Step-by-step instructions belong in numbered lists, and comparisons belong in tables. Structured formats are easier for both people and machines to extract correctly than the same information written as a long paragraph.

6. Show who wrote it and when

A visible author name, a short line on why they're qualified, and a published or updated date all signal that the page is maintained by someone accountable. Update dates matter most for anything that changes, such as prices, tools and regulations. If you update a page, update the date, and don't change the date if you didn't.

7. Don't block the crawlers you want to be cited by

Some hosting and security services can block AI crawlers by default, and some site owners block them deliberately. That's a legitimate business choice, but make it on purpose. Check your robots.txt and any bot settings in your hosting or CDN dashboard so you know which tools can actually read your pages.

What not to do

  • Don't stuff in AI-related keywords. Writing "ChatGPT" ten times doesn't make a page more likely to be cited by ChatGPT.
  • Don't pad pages with fake FAQ sections. Questions nobody asks, answered with filler, make the whole page look less trustworthy.
  • Don't hide text or write one version for bots and another for people. It's the oldest trick in search, and it still backfires.

None of this replaces the fundamentals. The page that gets cited is almost always the one that answers the question best. These steps just make sure that when you have the best answer, nothing stands between it and the reader.
