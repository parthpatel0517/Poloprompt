5 Automation Ideas Every Small Business Should Steal

Most small businesses don't need a custom-built software system — they need five or six small automations wired together with tools they can set up in an afternoon. Here are five that consistently save hours a week, regardless of industry, using nothing more exotic than Zapier, Make.com, Typeform, Google Sheets, Twilio, Calendly, Slack, or Mailchimp.

1. Missed-call text-back

Trigger: An incoming call goes unanswered on your business line.
Action: Twilio (or a Zapier phone integration) automatically sends the caller a text: "Sorry we missed you — how can we help?" with a link to book a time or reply directly.

Why it matters: a missed call is one of the highest-intent moments a business gets, and most of that intent evaporates within minutes if nobody follows up. An automatic text keeps the conversation alive without anyone having to drop what they're doing to call back immediately.

2. Review request after service completion

Trigger: A job, appointment, or order is marked complete in your calendar, CRM, or point-of-sale system.
Action: Zapier sends a text or email a few hours later with a direct link to leave a Google or Yelp review.

Why it matters: review requests sent while the experience is still fresh convert far better than a generic monthly ask, but almost nobody remembers to send them one at a time. Automating the timing means it happens consistently for every customer, not just the ones someone remembered to follow up with.

3. Lead form straight into your CRM (or a shared spreadsheet)

Trigger: Someone submits a Typeform or website contact form.
Action: Zapier or Make.com adds the lead as a new row in Google Sheets or a new contact in your CRM, and pings a Slack channel so the right person knows to follow up.

Why it matters: leads that sit in an inbox for a day are far less likely to convert than leads someone calls within the hour. Routing new leads into a visible, shared place — instead of one person's email — removes the single point of failure where a lead quietly goes cold because one person was busy.

4. Automated appointment reminders

Trigger: A new appointment is booked through Calendly.
Action: A text or email reminder is automatically sent 24 hours and again 2 hours before the appointment.

Why it matters: no-shows are one of the most expensive silent costs in any appointment-based business. A two-step reminder sequence is one of the cheapest ways to cut no-show rates, and once it's set up it runs forever without anyone touching it again.

5. Invoice and payment follow-up

Trigger: An invoice is marked unpaid past its due date in your invoicing tool.
Action: Zapier sends a polite automatic follow-up email (and optionally a Slack alert to whoever handles collections) at set intervals — say, 3 days, 10 days, and 20 days overdue.

Why it matters: chasing late payments is unpleasant enough that most people put it off, which is exactly why balances go uncollected for months. An automatic, scheduled nudge takes the emotional friction out of asking, and it asks exactly on time, every time, without anyone needing to remember.

Getting started

None of these require custom development — they're all buildable with the free or entry-level tiers of the tools mentioned, and most take under 30 minutes to set up once you know the trigger and action you want. The hard part is usually just deciding which one to build first; start with whichever process currently causes the most manual back-and-forth in your business.
