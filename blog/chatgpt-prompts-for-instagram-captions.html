ChatGPT Prompts for Instagram Captions That Don't Sound Like AI

You can usually spot an AI-written Instagram caption before you finish the first line. It opens with "Ready to elevate your morning routine?", stacks five emojis in a row, closes with "Who else can relate? 👇" and finishes with a wall of thirty hashtags. Every brand that uses it ends up sounding like every other brand that uses it.

That isn't really ChatGPT's fault. "Write an Instagram caption about our new candle" gives the model nothing to work with except the average of every caption it has ever seen, so the average is exactly what comes back. The fix is a better brief. Below are the five things every caption prompt needs, five prompts you can copy today, and a short edit pass that makes the output sound like you.

Why most AI captions fall flat

A caption has three jobs and very little room to do them. The opening line has to earn the tap on "more", because Instagram only shows the first line or two in the feed and everything after that is invisible unless the opening works. The body has to sound like a person rather than a press release. And the ending has to give the reader one obvious thing to do next.

A one-line prompt fails all three because it leaves out the information a good copywriter would ask for first: who is reading, how your brand talks, and what this particular post is supposed to achieve. Without those, the model fills the gaps with the safest, most generic choices it has.

What to put in every caption prompt

  • The audience in one sentence. "Busy parents buying gifts at the last minute" leads to different words, references and humor than "candle collectors who care about scent throw".
  • Your voice, plus the words you never use. Three adjectives help. A short banned-words list helps more, because it strips out the stock phrases (elevate, unleash, game-changer, "who else") that make captions sound generated.
  • The goal of this specific post. Saves, comments, shares, profile visits and link clicks each need a different call to action. Say which one you want.
  • One real detail. A number, a name, a mistake you made, something only you would know. Specifics are the fastest way to sound human.
  • Format limits. Set the number of lines, emojis and hashtags. If you don't, the model will over-decorate.

Prompt 1: The weekly batch writer

Use this when you have a week of posts planned and want every caption drafted in one go, all in the same voice.

You are the social media voice of [BRAND], a [WHAT YOU SELL] for [AUDIENCE].
Our voice: [3 ADJECTIVES]. Never use: [BANNED WORDS].
Write one caption for each post below.
- First line under 125 characters that works on its own in the feed.
- 2-4 short lines after it, with line breaks.
- One CTA that fits the post's goal (save, comment, share or link in bio).
- Max 2 emojis and 3-5 relevant hashtags.
- No two captions may start the same way.
Posts:
1. [POST IDEA + GOAL]
2. [POST IDEA + GOAL]
3. [POST IDEA + GOAL]

Prompt 2: Ten hooks to choose from

The first line matters more than everything else combined, so it's worth generating options instead of settling for the first one.

Give me 10 different opening lines for an Instagram post about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE].
Use a different technique for each: a specific number, a confession, a contrarian opinion, a question the audience already asks, the first line of a short story, a 'stop doing X' warning, a surprising before/after, a tiny how-to, a relatable complaint, and a bold prediction.
Under 15 words each. No exclamation marks and no emojis.

Prompt 3: Teach it your voice from your best captions

Adjectives like "friendly" mean different things to everyone. Your own past captions are a far more precise description of how you sound.

Here are 5 captions that performed well for us:
[PASTE CAPTIONS]
First, describe our voice in 5 bullet points: sentence length, humor, punctuation habits, vocabulary, and how we address the reader.
Then rewrite this draft caption in exactly that voice:
[PASTE DRAFT]

Prompt 4: Product launch caption in three tones

Write 3 versions of an Instagram caption announcing [PRODUCT] for [AUDIENCE]: one playful, one straightforward, one that opens with a short customer story.
Each version must lead with the problem the product solves, mention this specific detail: [DETAIL, e.g. price, launch date, ingredient], and end by telling people to tap the link in our bio.
Under 80 words each.

Prompt 5: A caption people actually comment on

"Thoughts?" rarely gets answers, because it asks the reader to do all the work. Easy, specific questions get far more replies.

Write an Instagram caption about [TOPIC] that ends with one specific, easy-to-answer question, such as a choice between two options or a one-word answer.
Don't use 'Thoughts?', 'Who else?' or 'Agree?'.
Give me 3 options for the closing question and explain which one is easiest to answer and why.

Edit for two minutes before you post

Even a well-briefed draft improves with a quick human pass:

  • Read the first line on its own. Would you stop scrolling for it?
  • Swap at least one general phrase for a specific detail: a number, a name, a place.
  • Delete any sentence that could appear on any other brand's account unchanged.
  • Check every fact, price and date. AI will confidently make these up.
  • Keep only hashtags someone would genuinely search for or browse.
